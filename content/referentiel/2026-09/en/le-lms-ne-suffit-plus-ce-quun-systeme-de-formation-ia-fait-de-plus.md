> An LMS, or learning management system, was designed to distribute content and track who has completed it. It fulfils that function. By design, it does not produce a different learning path for each profile, nor proof that a skill has been acquired, nor updates to the content. These three gaps are not the result of poor configuration but of the category of tool. An AI training system adds what distribution does not do: generating the learning path from the measured gap, measuring acquisition, and recalculating what comes next at each step.

### What an LMS does well

An LMS centralises content, manages enrolments, distributes modules to the right populations and produces activity reports: logins, time spent, completion rate. For a distribution obligation, mandatory training that everyone must follow, or a catalogue to make available, it remains the appropriate tool. The criticism levelled at it rarely concerns what it does, but what is expected of it and what it was not designed to do.

### Three category limits

**Personalisation remains manual.** An LMS distributes the same module to an entire population, or variants designed by hand through conditional paths defined in advance. Producing a distinct learning path for each profile, matched to what the person already masters, requires design work that does not hold up at the scale of hundreds of employees.

**Completion is not acquisition.** The completion rate shows that a person has been exposed to content. It does not say whether they can use the target skill. An LMS's reports answer the question "did the training take place", not "was the skill acquired". The subject is covered in [My LMS is useless: why completion rates collapse and how to fix it](/referentiel/mon-lms-ne-sert-a-rien-pourquoi-les-taux-de-completion-s-effondrent).

**Content ages.** A module produced once goes out of date at the pace of its subject. Updating it depends on internal or external producers, with lead times incompatible with fast-moving fields such as the uses of AI.

### What an AI training system adds

An AI training system starts from the target skills and each person's actual level. It generates the learning path that closes the gap, without making the learner go back over what they already master. It measures acquisition throughout the learning path, through applied assessments spread over time, and recalculates what comes next at each step based on verified learning. Finally, it provides skill-by-skill traceability that can be used for management, for a funding body or to demonstrate compliance.

The difference is therefore not one more feature, but a change of function: from distributing to generating and measuring.

### Replace or complement

The question "should we replace our LMS" is often asked too early. An AI training system can be integrated with an existing LMS via API, which continues to handle access and distribution, or operate on its own depending on the infrastructure in place. The right criterion is not the current tool but what the organisation needs to produce: if the need is measured acquisition and individualised learning paths at scale, distribution alone does not cover it. ### Frequently asked questions

**Is an LMS with AI features an AI training system?**
Not necessarily. If the AI is limited to recommending existing content or helping to create it, the function remains distribution. The decisive capabilities are generating the learning path from the measured gap and measuring acquisition.

**Do you need to abandon your LMS to move to an AI training system?**
No. The two can coexist: the LMS handles access and distribution, while the AI training system generates learning paths and measures acquisition.

**Why is the completion rate not enough?**
Because it measures exposure to content, not ability. A person can finish a module without being able to apply the target skill.

**What signs show that an LMS has reached its limits?**
High completion rates with no visible effect on the ground, identical learning paths for very different profiles, content that takes months to update, and an inability to document acquisition for a funding body or an auditor.

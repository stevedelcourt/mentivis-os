> Looking for the best LMS often means comparing tools that share the same function, managing and delivering training, without questioning that function itself. The major players on the French market, 360Learning, Docebo, Moodle and Rise Up, differ in experience, coverage or positioning, but belong to the same family: they distribute content and track activity. An AI training engine belongs to another category: it generates the learning path and measures acquisition. The right choice does not depend on a ranking, but on what actually needs to be produced.

### The landscape, described by category

Based on their public positioning, these players fall into complementary registers. 360Learning emphasises collaborative learning and combines the LMS and LXP approaches. Docebo is an enterprise LMS with a strong international presence that integrates AI features for recommendation and content creation. Moodle is the open-source reference, adaptable to the needs of in-house technical teams. Rise Up is a French provider focused on upskilling, which has built adaptive learning natively into its platform. Others, such as Apolearn and Beedeez, specialise respectively in training organisations and in mobile field teams.

These descriptions are not value judgements. They place offerings within a single market, that of managing and delivering training.

### The question that really decides

A feature-by-feature comparison hides the real dividing line. An LMS, however good, answers the question of delivery: how to make content available and track who has taken it. It does not, on its own, answer three distinct questions. How to produce a different learning path for each profile without designing it by hand. How to verify that the target skill has been acquired, and not merely that the module has been completed. How to keep content up to date without depending on external producers.

These three questions define another category of tool, described in [The LMS is no longer enough: what an AI training system does beyond it](/referentiel/le-lms-ne-suffit-plus-ce-quun-systeme-de-formation-ia-fait-de-plus) and distinguished from the other families in [LMS, LXP, adaptive learning, AI training engine: how to tell them apart](/referentiel/lms-lxp-adaptive-learning-moteur-de-formation-ia-comment-les-distinguer).

### What an AI training engine brings

An AI training engine generates the learning path from the target skills and the profile, measures acquisition skill by skill, and recalculates what comes next at each step. It can integrate with an existing LMS via API to add these capabilities, or operate as a replacement. The right framing is therefore not "replace my LMS with a better LMS", but "add generation and measurement where my LMS stops".

### Frequently asked questions

**Should you abandon your current LMS?**
Not necessarily. An AI training engine can complement an existing LMS via API or replace it, depending on the infrastructure.

**Why not simply choose the top-ranked LMS?**
Because a ranking compares tools of the same category. If the need is measured acquisition and personalisation at scale, no LMS covers it on its own.

**These platforms have AI features, is that equivalent?**
It depends on what the AI does. Recommending or creating content is not the same as generating an individualised learning path and measuring acquisition skill by skill.

**How can you compare objectively?**
By asking for a demonstration on your own frameworks and your own profiles, not on an ideal case prepared by the vendor. The questions to ask are listed in [How to choose an AI training system](/referentiel/comment-choisir-un-systeme-de-formation-ia-les-bonnes-questions-a-poser).

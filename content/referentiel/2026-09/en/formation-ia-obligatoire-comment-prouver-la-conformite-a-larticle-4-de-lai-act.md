> AI training is mandatory for every organisation that uses AI systems, under Article 4 of the European AI regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). The obligation is not simply a matter of having people attend a training course. It requires ensuring that the people concerned genuinely master the tools they use, and being able to demonstrate this in the event of an inspection or an incident. The real issue is therefore not training, but producing documented proof that the training had a measurable effect, skill by skill. This traceability is precisely what most training programmes do not produce.

### What Article 4 actually requires

Article 4, entitled "AI literacy", covers providers and deployers of AI systems. A deployer is any organisation that uses an AI system under its own authority, in a professional context. A company whose teams use a generative assistant, a decision-support tool or an AI feature built into business software falls within this scope.

The obligation has applied since February 2025. The text was clarified in 2026: it is no longer framed around a "sufficient level" of proficiency guaranteed for each individual, but the obligation to act remains in full. The regulation does not attach a specific fine to Article 4, but each Member State may penalise a breach, and the absence of documented training weakens the organisation's position as soon as an incident occurs, for example a data leak via an AI assistant or a flawed decision based on unverified output.

### Training is not enough, it must be demonstrated

Documented training is the best proof of compliance. Yet most programmes record attendance or module completion, not real proficiency. This distinction is decisive. Completion proves that a person has been exposed to content. It does not prove that they can recognise the limits of a tool, protect confidential data or check generated output.

In an inspection by a national authority, or in a defence following an incident, the question will not be "did you train?" but "can you demonstrate that the training produced proficiency suited to actual uses?". A completion rate does not answer that question. A measurement of acquisition, skill by skill, does.

### What usable traceability contains

Solid proof of training within the meaning of Article 4 rests on four elements. A map of AI uses by profile and by department, identifying who must be trained and in what. A measurement of acquisition built into the learning path, checking the ability to use the tool and not merely to recite its definition. A summary of acquired skills per person, time-stamped and usable by an auditor. A link with an AI use charter, whose signature at the end of the learning path formalises the employee's commitment.

These elements turn an imposed obligation into a risk management system. They make compliance demonstrable rather than declarative. ### Funding compliance

Training teams in AI falls within the scope of continuing professional training. It is therefore eligible for the funding schemes of the skills operators, subject to Qualiopi compliance. Depending on the scope and the OPCO concerned, a significant share of the cost may be covered. ### Frequently asked questions

**Is a single general awareness session enough to be compliant?**
No. Article 4 expects proficiency suited to the context, and therefore calibrated to the actual uses of each profile. Uniform awareness training does not cover a deployer whose functions use AI in different ways.

**Is the completion rate proof of proficiency?**
No. It attests to exposure to content, not acquisition. The expected proof concerns demonstrated ability, measured skill by skill.

**Can AI training be funded?**
Yes, subject to conditions. It falls under continuing professional training and can be covered by an OPCO if the Qualiopi compliance requirements are met.

**What should an Article 4 compliance file contain?**
The map of uses, the record of learning paths completed, the measurement of acquisition per person, a time-stamped summary of acquired skills, and the link with an AI use charter.

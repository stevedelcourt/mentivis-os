> Most training programmes measure attendance and completion, that is, exposure to training, not its effect. Measuring real acquisition, skill by skill, changes what management can do with the data, what is produced for funders, and what employees can make of their progress. This pillar brings together what real measurement covers, how it feeds a living skills map, and what it changes for L&D.

### What real measurement covers

Measuring acquisition relies on assessments spread across the learning path, staggered over time, and applied, testing the ability to use a skill and not merely to recognise it. The substance of the topic is covered in [Measuring skills acquisition: beyond completion rate](/referentiel/la-mesure-de-lacquisition-de-competences-au-dela-du-taux-de-completion) and [What real skills measurement is and why it changes everything for HR management](/referentiel/quest-ce-que-la-mesure-reelle-des-competences-et-pourquoi-elle-change-tout).

### From measurement to a living map

Continuous measurement feeds a map that updates as employees progress, instead of freezing an annual snapshot. The continuity between the measured gap and the learning path that closes it is covered in [Skills mapping: from measuring the gap to the learning path that closes it](/referentiel/cartographie-des-competences-de-la-mesure-de-lecart-au-parcours-qui-le-comble).

### What it changes for management

Management shifts from attendance to skills. See [How an HR Director steers skills with AI](/referentiel/comment-un-drh-pilote-les-competences-avec-lia).

### Frequently asked questions

**Is an end-of-module quiz enough?**
It measures short-term memorisation. Measuring lasting acquisition requires assessments spread over time and practical scenarios.

**Can this data be shared with the employee?**
Yes, and it is advisable. Seeing their progress skill by skill strengthens engagement.

**What obligations apply to this data?**
Employee training data is personal data protected by the GDPR, with the corresponding obligations.

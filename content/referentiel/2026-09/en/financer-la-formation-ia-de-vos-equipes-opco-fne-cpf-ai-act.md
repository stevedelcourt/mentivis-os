> AI training falls within continuing vocational training. As such, it can draw on several funding schemes depending on who is being trained and in what context: the skills operators (OPCO) for the company's skills development plan, the FNE-Formation scheme in certain situations, and the personal training account (CPF) for individual initiatives. Funding depends on conditions, first among them the provider's compliance with the Qualiopi framework. The AI training obligation introduced by Article 4 of the AI Act makes this funding all the more relevant.

### The schemes available

The skills operators fund all or part of the company's skills development plan, subject to conditions specific to each OPCO and each sector. The FNE-Formation scheme may apply in certain situations, notably business transformation or change in activity, with terms that change from one year to the next. The personal training account is intended for individual initiatives by working people. These schemes cannot be combined freely: they follow distinct rationales and serve distinct beneficiaries.

### The central condition: Qualiopi

Funding by funders requires, in most cases, that the provider complies with the Qualiopi framework. This compliance attests to a quality approach and to the ability to demonstrate that learning objectives have been achieved. It is also why detailed traceability of what has been learned is gaining importance: a funder expects proof of delivery and of results, not merely proof of attendance.

### The effect of Article 4 of the AI Act

Article 4 of the AI Act requires organisations that use AI systems to ensure that their teams have command of these tools. This obligation brings AI training within the scope of vocational training, and therefore within the scope of funding schemes, under the same conditions. In other words, a compliance obligation can be addressed in part through existing funding, rather than from the direct budget, when the conditions are met. The precise terms vary and should be checked with your OPCO. The expected method of proof is detailed in [Mandatory AI training: how to prove compliance with Article 4](/referentiel/formation-ia-obligatoire-comment-prouver-la-conformite-a-larticle-4-de-lai-act), and the full overview of schemes in [Funding corporate training: the complete guide](/referentiel/financement-de-la-formation-en-entreprise-cpf-opco-fne-pro-a-le-guide-complet).

### What secures a funding application

A solid application rests on three elements. A provider that complies with the Qualiopi framework. Learning objectives defined in terms of skills, not hours. Traceability of acquisition that documents the result, skill by skill, and not merely completion. This last point is what distinguishes a fragile application from a robust one in the event of an audit.

### What MentivisOS brings to funding

[MentivisOS Entreprise](/entreprises) and [MentivisOS Education](/education) natively produce traceability of acquisition skill by skill, directly usable for funding applications and for the reporting expected by funders. They do not provide funding themselves, but they supply the documented proof on which obtaining and securing it depends.

### Frequently asked questions

**Can AI training be funded by an OPCO?**
Yes, subject to conditions specific to each OPCO and each sector, and provided that the provider complies with the Qualiopi framework.

**Does the CPF fund AI training in companies?**
The personal training account is intended for individual initiatives by working people. For a collective company plan, it is the OPCO schemes and, in some cases, FNE-Formation that apply.

**Can AI Act compliance be funded?**
Training that brings an organisation into compliance under Article 4 falls within vocational training and can draw on these schemes, under the same conditions. The terms should be checked with your OPCO.

**Why does traceability matter for funding?**
Because a funder expects proof of results, not merely of attendance. Measuring acquisition skill by skill strengthens the application.

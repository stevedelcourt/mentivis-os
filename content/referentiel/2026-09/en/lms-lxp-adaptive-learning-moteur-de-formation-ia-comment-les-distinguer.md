> LMS, LXP, adaptive learning and AI training engine refer to four families of tools that partly overlap and are often confused in sales pitches. The LMS manages and distributes training. The LXP organises access to varied content according to the learner's interests. Adaptive learning adjusts a learning path to the learner's performance. The AI training engine generates the learning path from the measured skills gap and measures acquisition. Distinguishing them by their function, rather than by their label, avoids buying one category in place of another.

### The LMS: managing and distributing

The LMS (Learning Management System) centralises content, manages enrolments and obligations, distributes modules and reports on activity: logins, time spent, completion rate. Its logic is administrative: getting the right people to follow the right training and documenting it. It is suited to mandatory training, catalogues and session management.

### The LXP: organising access to content

The LXP (Learning Experience Platform) focuses on the learner's experience. It aggregates content from multiple sources, internal and external, and recommends it according to profile, interests or usage, on the model of a content platform. Its logic is discovery and engagement. It does not, as such, verify that a skill has been acquired.

### Adaptive learning: adjusting the learning path

Adaptive learning refers to a method rather than a category of tool: adjusting the content, difficulty and order of a learning path to the learner's actual performance. Depending on the implementation, the adjustment ranges from conditional paths defined in advance to recalculating the learning path at each step. Its effectiveness depends on this implementation, as detailed in [Adaptive learning in the workplace: what really works](/referentiel/adaptive-learning-en-entreprise-ce-qui-marche-vraiment).

### The AI training engine: generating and measuring

The AI training engine starts from the gap between the target skills and the skills actually acquired. It generates the learning path that closes this gap for each person, measures acquisition throughout the learning path and recalculates what comes next based on verified learning. It incorporates adaptation as a mechanism, but its own function is producing the learning path and proving acquisition, skill by skill.

### Why the categories blur

Vendors add features from one category to another: recommendations in an LMS, adaptive learning paths in an LXP, AI content generation almost everywhere. The vocabulary follows, and the same term covers different realities. The useful test concerns function: does the tool distribute existing content, or does it produce a learning path from a measured gap? Does it verify completion, or acquisition?

### How to choose

The choice follows from what the organisation needs to produce. To document mandatory training, an LMS is sufficient. To develop a culture of self-directed learning, an LXP is relevant. To upskill hundreds of people on specific objectives and demonstrate it, you need to generate and measure. ### Frequently asked questions

**Does an LXP replace an LMS?**
Not always. The LXP organises access to content, while the LMS manages obligations and distribution. Many organisations use both.

**Is adaptive learning a type of software?**
It is first and foremost a method. It is found in tools from different categories, with very varied degrees of implementation.

**What distinguishes an AI training engine from an LMS with AI?**
The function. An LMS with AI remains an enhanced distribution tool. An AI training engine generates the learning path from the measured gap and measures acquisition.

**Can these tools work together?**
Yes. An AI training engine can be integrated with an LMS or an LXP via API, which continue to handle access and distribution.

> The decision whether to train in-house or to hire AI profiles depends on the nature of the skills required, the time available and the budget. For usage skills (using AI in a business context), in-house training is consistently faster and less expensive. For development skills (designing, training or deploying AI systems), hiring is often unavoidable in the short term, but training can take over in the medium term.

### Why the question arises in 2026

The market for AI profiles is under structural strain. Demand for data scientists, machine learning engineers and AI product managers exceeds the available supply in almost every European country. Hiring takes a long time, salaries have risen by 20 to 40% in three years, and retention of these profiles is low.

At the same time, the range of AI training on offer has grown considerably. Programmes of varying quality, ranging from a few hours to several months, make it possible to upskill existing employees on specific uses.

Most organisations need both, but not in the same proportions or for the same scopes.

### The decision framework

Three questions help to structure the decision.

**Question 1: are these usage skills or technical skills?**

AI usage skills correspond to the ability to use existing AI tools to improve productivity in a given business context. Writing with an AI assistant, analysing data with augmented BI tools, using a business chatbot: these are usage skills. They are acquired through training, often in a few days to a few weeks.

AI technical skills correspond to the ability to design, train, fine-tune or deploy AI systems. Data science, MLOps, LLM engineering: these are skills that require lengthy training (6 to 24 months to reach an operational level) or hiring.

**Question 2: how much time is available?**

If the organisation needs the skill within 3 to 6 months, hiring is the only realistic lever for technical skills. Training, even intensive training, does not produce results within that timeframe for complex skills.

If the organisation has a horizon of 12 to 24 months, in-house training becomes competitive for almost all usage profiles, and for some technical profiles.

**Question 3: what is the lifespan of the skill required?**

A profile hired to master a specific tool will potentially be obsolete within 2 to 3 years if that tool evolves or disappears. Training an existing employee in the general principles of AI use, rather than in a particular tool, produces a more durable skill.

### Comparison of cost, time and risk

| Criterion | In-house training | External hiring |
|---|---|---|
| Cost (usage skills) | 500 to 3,000 euros per person | Not applicable |
| Cost (technical skills) | 5,000 to 30,000 euros per person (long programme) | 60,000 to 120,000 euros annual package + recruitment cost of 15 to 25% |
| Time to productivity | 1 to 3 months (usage) / 12 to 24 months (technical) | 3 to 9 months (hiring + onboarding) |
| Risk of departure | Low (retention improved by the investment) | High (very liquid market for AI profiles) |
| Fit with the business context | Strong (existing working relationship) | Variable (learning curve on the context) |

### What organisations that have handled this question well have in common

They started with usage skills, not technical skills. Training 100 employees to use the available AI tools effectively produces more value in the short term than hiring 2 technical profiles.

They identified internal "AI champions" before hiring externally. Almost every organisation already has employees who use AI in an advanced way, personally or professionally. Identifying them, equipping them and positioning them as internal relays is the fastest and least expensive lever.

They were honest about what they could not train for in-house. Certain specific technical skills (LLM architecture, large-scale MLOps) genuinely require hiring or the use of an external provider.

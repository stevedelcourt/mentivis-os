> Choosing an AI training system requires assessing four dimensions: functional (does the system really generate learning paths or does it select from a catalogue?), technical (HRIS integration, data hosting, the language model used), legal (GDPR, AI Act, Qualiopi) and organisational (deployment time, maintenance workload, support from the vendor). The most important distinction is between genuine dynamic personalisation and personalisation through predefined conditional paths: these two approaches carry the same commercial name but produce very different results.

### Why this market is hard to read

The market for AI training systems is growing quickly and is very poorly standardised. The same promise (personalised learning paths, adaptive AI, skills measurement) covers very different technical realities from one vendor to another. Sales demonstrations are built on ideal cases.

A rigorous choice depends on being able to distinguish what has been demonstrated from what has been announced.

### The functional questions

**Does the system generate content or only distribute it?**
An AI system that generates content from a skills framework is fundamentally different from an LMS that distributes existing content. The ability to generate is what allows it to adapt to specific needs without depending on a pre-existing library.

**Is the personalisation dynamic or based on predefined paths?**
Personalisation through conditional paths ("if the learner answers incorrectly, they go to module B") is a fixed branching structure. Dynamic personalisation recalculates the learning path after each assessment according to what has actually been acquired. The demonstration should let you see this second mechanism at work on a real case.

**Is measurement of acquisition built in or added at the end of the learning path?**
A multiple-choice quiz at the end of a module measures short-term memorisation. Built-in measurement assesses skill by skill at points spread over time and triggers an adjustment of the learning path.

**Can the system incorporate your existing skills frameworks?**
Ask how long this takes and who does it.

### The technical questions

HRIS and LMS integration: which APIs are available and in production use with existing clients? Data hosting: on what infrastructure, in which country? Language model: which one is used, and can it be audited? Availability: what is the SLA under real load conditions?

### The legal questions

GDPR: is the data processing documented in a processing agreement that complies with Article 28? AI Act: are automated decisions affecting learners taken without human oversight? Qualiopi: how does the system produce the data needed to report on outcome indicators?

### The organisational questions

Actual deployment time and cost (figures observed at similar clients, not sales estimates). Configuration and maintenance workload: who updates the frameworks? Day-to-day management: who in the organisation runs the platform?

### What to avoid

Choosing on the basis of a demonstration using a case prepared by the vendor. Insist on a demonstration using your own data and frameworks. Choosing on the basis of the number of features announced without checking which ones are operational in production. Choosing without involving the IT department, the HR director and the CFO.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is a call for tenders necessary?**
Depending on the organisation and the amounts involved, it may be mandatory. Regardless of any obligation, it provides a useful structure for the comparison.

**How do you assess the soundness of a young vendor?**
Client references that have been in production for at least a year, documented case studies with measured indicators, and information on the financial structure.

**Is a pilot recommended before a full deployment?**
Yes, in every case. A pilot on a limited scope (50 to 200 learners) reveals the real problems that the demonstration does not show.

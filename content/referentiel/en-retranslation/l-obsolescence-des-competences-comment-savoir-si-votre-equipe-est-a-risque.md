> The average lifespan of a technical skill has fallen from 30 years in the 1980s to less than 5 years today, according to World Economic Forum data. For skills related to digital technology and AI, this lifespan can be less than 18 months. An organisation that does not monitor the obsolescence of its skills portfolio is taking a measurable operational risk, regardless of its investment in training.

### What the data says

Since 2016 the World Economic Forum has published its report on the future of jobs (Future of Jobs Report). The 2025 edition estimates that 44% of workers' skills will be disrupted in the next 5 years, and that the pace of obsolescence is accelerating with the spread of generative AI.

The OECD, for its part, has documented in its work on "skills for the future" that the jobs most exposed to automation are not only low-skilled jobs. Some intermediate-skilled jobs in the service sector (information processing, data entry, standard document analysis) are more exposed than manual jobs requiring dexterity or contextual judgement.

The IBM Institute for Business Value estimated in 2024 that 40% of the global workforce will need to be reskilled over the next 3 years because of AI.

### What is the "half-life" of a skill?

The concept of the half-life of skills comes from research in the educational sciences. It refers to the time after which half of what a person has learned in a field becomes obsolete or insufficient to work at the expected level.

In the 1980s, the half-life of an engineering degree was estimated at 10 to 15 years. In 2026, it is estimated at less than 5 years for technical skills, and less than 2 years for skills related to specific digital tools.

This notion has a direct implication for those responsible for training: a training plan that renews skills on a 3 to 5 year cycle is structurally behind the obsolescence curve.

### How to identify at-risk skills in your organisation

Three criteria make it possible to assess a skill's exposure to obsolescence.

The degree of routinisation. Skills that consist of applying a rule or procedure to structured data are the most exposed to automation. Data entry, compliance checks against fixed criteria and the generation of standardised documents fall into this category.

The degree of dependence on a specific tool. A skill defined by mastery of a particular piece of software is exposed to every major change in that tool. Skills defined by the ability to solve a type of problem, whatever the tool, are more durable.

The volume of output that AI can substitute. If generative AI produces in 2 minutes an output of comparable quality to what an employee used to produce in 2 hours, the value of that skill within the organisation has mechanically decreased, even if the skill of supervising and evaluating the output has increased.

### The diagnostic tool: the skills/exposure matrix

A simple exercise consists of listing the organisation's 20 to 30 key skills (those whose absence would paralyse operations) and assessing them on two axes: the current level of mastery in the team (from 1 to 5) and the level of exposure to obsolescence within 3 years (from 1 to 5).

Skills in the "low mastery / high exposure" quadrant are the absolute priorities for the training plan. Skills in the "high mastery / high exposure" quadrant are the priorities for internal reskilling.

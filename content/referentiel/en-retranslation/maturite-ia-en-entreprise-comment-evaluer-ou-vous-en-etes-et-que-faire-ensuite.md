> An organisation's AI maturity is measured on five levels, from level 1 (no structured use of AI) to level 5 (an organisation optimised by AI, in a continuous learning loop). In 2026, most French companies sit between level 2 (uncoordinated experimentation) and level 3 (controlled deployment on a few processes). The assessment rests on four dimensions: actual use, skills in place, governance and culture.

### Why assess your AI maturity before taking any other action?

Most AI projects in companies fail not for lack of available technology, but because of a mismatch between the project's ambition and the organisation's real maturity. Deploying an advanced AI tool in an organisation that has not yet stabilised its basic processes is like putting a Formula 1 engine in a car whose brakes have not been checked.

Assessing AI maturity serves three practical purposes. It avoids buying tools that go unused. It makes it possible to prioritise training investment. It provides a documented starting point for tracking progress.

### The 5 levels of AI maturity

**Level 1: Non-existent.** No AI system is used intentionally. Employees may use personal tools on an individual basis, without oversight. There is no usage policy, no training and no governance. This is the situation that is exposed to the AI Act Article 4 obligation without even being aware of it.

**Level 2: Experimentation.** AI tools are used occasionally by some employees, usually on their own initiative. Uses are disparate, undocumented and not assessed for results. There is no usage charter. This level corresponds to "shadow AI": the company uses AI, but it does not really know it.

**Level 3: Controlled deployment.** The company has identified 2 to 5 priority use cases and deployed tools in these areas in a supervised way. There is a usage charter, at least one awareness session, and a designated point of contact. Results are measured informally.

**Level 4: Systemic integration.** AI is integrated into several key business processes. AI skills are developed in a structured way, with learning paths differentiated by profile. There is formal governance (an AI committee or equivalent), performance indicators, and monitoring of regulatory developments.

**Level 5: Optimised organisation.** AI is a fundamental organisational capability, not a project. AI processes are in a continuous improvement loop. The organisation produces its own learning data, systematically assesses its tools and is able to evolve its deployments autonomously.

### How to assess yourself: the four dimensions

**Dimension 1: actual use.** Which AI tools are actually used in the organisation, by how many people, for which tasks? An honest 30-minute inventory often reveals that the organisation is more advanced (or more disorganised) than it thinks.

**Dimension 2: skills in place.** What percentage of employees have received AI training in the last 12 months? How able are teams to assess an AI output, detect a hallucination, write an effective prompt?

**Dimension 3: governance.** Is there a policy on the use of AI tools? A designated person in charge? Clear rules on which data may be entered into external LLMs?

**Dimension 4: culture.** Is AI perceived as a threat or as a tool? Are individual initiatives discouraged or valued? Does the organisation communicate about its AI experiences, including failures?

### What to do depending on your level

If you are at level 1 or 2, the priority is governance and awareness, before any technology investment. A review of existing uses, a simple usage charter and a two-hour awareness session for all employees are enough to move to level 3, and to meet the AI Act Article 4 obligation.

If you are at level 3, the priority is differentiating training learning paths and measuring impact. Training everyone in the same way is the main cause of stagnation at this level.

If you are at level 4, the priority is data integration and model governance. Questions of data sovereignty, traceability of AI decisions and continuous assessment of tools become central.

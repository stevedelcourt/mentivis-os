> The average completion rate for online training in companies lies between 5% and 20%, depending on the study and the sector. This figure is systematically underestimated by L&D teams, who measure the administrative completion rate (the module has been "completed" in the system) and not the real completion rate. The main cause is not learners' lack of motivation: it is the disconnect between the content of the training and real work situations.

### The uncomfortable figure

In 2019 MIT documented that online MOOCs have completion rates of between 3% and 6% on average, including for courses from major universities with high-quality content. Corporate e-learning, although shorter and more targeted, does little better: sector studies converge on real completion rates of between 10% and 25% for non-mandatory training.

This figure means that, for every euro invested in producing an e-learning module, between 75 and 90% of that investment has no learning effect. It is a structural inefficiency that affects almost all LMS deployments.

### The real causes of the problem

**The content is too long.** An adult's sustained attention on a screen is estimated at between 6 and 20 minutes, depending on the study. Corporate e-learning modules generally last between 30 minutes and 2 hours. Length is the leading cause of drop-out.

**The content is too generic.** A module designed to be valid for as many participants as possible is, by construction, of little relevance to each of them in particular. A learner who follows a module on "time management" with no link to their own tasks and their own context sees no immediate use in what they are learning.

**There is no consequence for not finishing.** In most organisations, non-mandatory e-learning is optional in practice. The manager does not track their team's completion rates, or tracks them only for regulatory compliance. The absence of consequence (positive or negative) is the second cause of drop-out.

**The timing of training is wrong.** Training in a block, outside the context of application, produces little retention. Training at the moment the need is active (just before carrying out a newly learned task, for example) is exponentially more effective.

### What works

Training programmes that achieve completion rates above 70% generally share three characteristics. The modules are short (5 to 15 minutes). They are directly linked to a real and recent work situation. And they are followed by an immediate opportunity to apply what was learned, documented and recognised by the manager.

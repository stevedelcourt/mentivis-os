> Real skills measurement consists of verifying what an employee is able to do, skill by skill, rather than recording their attendance at a training session or their completion of a module. This distinction changes three things: what HR management can do with training data (moving from passive reporting to active management), what can be produced for funders (fine-grained traceability that can be used in OPCO funding applications), and what employees themselves can put forward (measured progress, not a list of courses attended).

### What traditional systems measure

The training data produced by traditional systems answers a single question: did the training take place? Attendance, duration, completion rates, multiple-choice quiz scores: none of these data answers the question "has the target skill been acquired, and can it be applied in real work?"

### What a well-designed system measures

A system that genuinely measures skills produces data on three dimensions.

Acquisition skill by skill: not an overall module score, but a level of acquisition for each target skill, assessed through representative exercises and situations.

Retention over time: a skill assessed immediately after training and a skill assessed six weeks later produce different data. Delayed measurement is the only kind that reveals whether acquisition is lasting.

Transfer to real situations: does the employee actually use the skill in their real work? This dimension often requires observation by a manager or a structured self-assessment.

### What this changes for HR management

A training department equipped with data on real acquisition can identify the skills that are well acquired and those that resist, measure the gap between the objectives of the strategic plan and the actual situation, and compare the effectiveness of different training arrangements on the basis of measured data rather than completion rates.

### What this changes for compliance

Funders increasingly require traceability that goes beyond the completion rate. A system that natively produces acquisition data skill by skill reduces the documentation workload for funding applications and makes them more robust during audits.

### What this changes for the employee

An employee who has a summary of what they have acquired, skill by skill, can put their progress forward in their professional development reviews and when seeking internal mobility. This visibility is documented as a factor in engagement with learning.

### Frequently asked questions

**Isn't a multiple-choice quiz at the end of a module enough?**
It measures short-term memorisation. Measuring lasting acquisition requires assessments spread over time and practical scenarios.

**Can this data be shared with the employee?**
Yes, and it is desirable. Sharing must respect the GDPR rules that apply to training data.

**What legal obligations apply to this data?**
Employees' training data is personal data protected by the GDPR. Its collection, processing and retention period must be documented and compliant.

> Finance departments approach training as a cost to be contained. AI in training changes this analytical framework: it makes measurable an investment that was not measurable before. An AI system produces data that make it possible to reason in terms of return on investment: less training time through the removal of content already mastered (documented gains of 20 to 40% on comparable scopes), fewer repeated courses, traceability that secures OPCO funding, and measurement of skills progression against operational indicators.

### Why the CFO resists training

Training is perceived as a discretionary cost for three structural reasons. Its return is not measured: the accounts record the expenditure but not its yield. Its impact is deferred: the effects appear at 3, 6 or 12 months, outside the usual reporting cycles. Its link with results is difficult to isolate without a control group.

### What AI changes in the calculation

An AI system changes three variables.

It reduces training time through personalisation. A learner who only works through the content that corresponds to their real gaps spends less time in training. On documented scopes, this reduction is 20 to 40%.

It reduces repeated courses. A system that measures real acquisition and adapts revision reduces the rate of retraining on subjects that were not sufficiently acquired.

It produces traceability that secures OPCO funding. Funding applications rejected for insufficient documentation represent a real loss of income.

It makes skills progression measurable against operational indicators. For the first time, it becomes possible to correlate progression on a skill with an operational performance indicator.

### How to build the financial case

The most convincing method for a CFO is to start from a limited, documented and comparable scope. Identify a precise scope (one team, one or two priority skills). Measure the current training costs on this scope: learning costs, salary cost of training hours, cost of repeated courses, cost of non-compliance. Project the savings: a 20% reduction in training time (the low, prudent threshold), a 50% reduction in repeated courses, OPCO funding gains. Compare with the cost of the system over 3 years.

### What to avoid

Comparing the cost of the platform with the current training budget. The return is assessed over the whole cycle: production cost, employee time, effectiveness of acquisition, compliance. Presenting a single ROI figure without a range: a presentation with a low range, a central hypothesis and a high range is more credible.

### Frequently asked questions

**How can you obtain reference data?**
Serious vendors produce documented case studies. Specialist firms (Josh Bersin Company, Fosway Group in Europe) publish sector analyses.

**What is a realistic payback period?**
Between 6 and 18 months for a well-targeted deployment, depending on the maturity of the organisation.

**Does OPCO funding cover AI platforms?**
The terms vary by OPCO and by sector. You need to check directly with your OPCO.

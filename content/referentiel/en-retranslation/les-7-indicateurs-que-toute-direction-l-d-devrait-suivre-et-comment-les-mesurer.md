> L&D teams are often judged on volume indicators (number of training hours, number of participants) that measure activity, not impact. The 7 indicators that make it possible to genuinely manage a training function are: the rate of application of learning at 90 days, the pre/post-training skills delta, the coverage rate of critical skills, the cost per hour of effective (not delivered) training, the adjusted completion rate, learner NPS, and the strategic alignment of the training portfolio.

### Why volume indicators are not enough

The number of training hours delivered, the number of trainees trained, the training budget spent: these indicators measure the investment, not the return. They tell you whether training took place, not whether it had an effect.

This confusion between activity and impact is the main reason training budgets are vulnerable when money is tight: if executive management cannot see the impact of training on the company's results, it cuts the training budget in the belief that it is eliminating an expense with nothing to show for it.

### The 7 indicators

**KPI 1: Rate of application of learning at 90 days.**
What it measures: the proportion of participants who actually apply in their work what they learned in training, 90 days after the end of the session.
How to measure it: a short questionnaire sent to participants and their managers 90 days after the training, with 3 to 5 questions on the changes in practice observed.
Target value: above 60% for high-stakes training.

**KPI 2: Pre/post-training skills delta.**
What it measures: the difference between the skill level before and after the training, measured against the stated learning objectives.
How to measure it: a skills test before the training (pre-test) and the same test afterwards (post-test). The delta is the measured progress.
Target value: varies by training; the aim is to define a minimum progress threshold in advance.

**KPI 3: Coverage rate of critical skills.**
What it measures: the proportion of employees who have reached the required level on the skills identified as critical for the organisation.
How to measure it: cross-referencing skills assessment data with the map of critical skills.
Target value: defined case by case according to strategic priorities.

**KPI 4: Cost per hour of effective training.**
What it measures: the cost of an hour of training that actually produced measurable learning, as opposed to the cost of an hour of training delivered.
How to measure it: divide the total cost of the training by the number of training hours that achieved the learning objectives (measured by the post-tests).
Why it matters: this indicator reveals that the training that is cheapest per hour is often the most expensive per effective hour.

**KPI 5: Adjusted completion rate.**
What it measures: the real completion rate of online training, distinguishing "administrative" completion (the module was opened and clicked through to the end) from real completion (the content was followed actively).
How to measure it: analysis of behaviour logs on the LMS platform, identifying very short sessions, fast-forward navigation and completions without interaction.

**KPI 6: Learner NPS.**
What it measures: participants' propensity to recommend the training to a colleague, on a scale of 0 to 10.
How to measure it: a single question at the end of each training course. A training NPS above 40 is considered good; above 60, excellent.
Why it matters: NPS is a more discriminating indicator of perceived quality than traditional satisfaction questionnaires.

**KPI 7: Strategic alignment of the portfolio.**
What it measures: the proportion of the training budget allocated to actions directly linked to the identified strategic priorities, as opposed to comfort, compliance or routine training.
How to measure it: categorise each training action in the PDC according to its link with the strategic priorities. Calculate the corresponding percentage of the budget.
Target value: at least 50% of the training budget should be directly aligned with strategic priorities.

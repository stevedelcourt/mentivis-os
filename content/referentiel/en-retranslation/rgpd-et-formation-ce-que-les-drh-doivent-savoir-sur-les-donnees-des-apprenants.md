> Online training platforms (LMSs) collect personal data about learners: identity, progress through modules, assessment results, time spent on each piece of content, and login data. This data is subject to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Companies that deploy an LMS have specific obligations regarding legal basis, retention period, learners' rights and information. The CNIL has issued several formal notices in this area since 2022.

### What data do LMSs collect?

A standard LMS collects several categories of data on each learner.

Identification data: surname, first name, work email address, HRIS identifier, department, job title, manager.

Learning behaviour data: date and time of login, time spent on each module, progress rate, number of attempts at assessments, score obtained, answers to questions (in systems that store them individually).

Performance data: post-test results, summaries of learning outcomes, progress against the skills framework, history of training completed.

In LMSs that incorporate AI features, additional data may be collected: analysis of navigation patterns, inferences about the learner's gaps, and personalised recommendations generated.

### The applicable legal framework

The GDPR (EU Regulation 2016/679) applies to any collection and processing of personal data concerning natural persons located in the European Union, wherever the processor (the LMS vendor) is located. The prior CNIL formalities were abolished by the GDPR, but the substantive obligations have been strengthened.

**The legal basis.** For employee training data, the legal basis most commonly relied on is the employer's legitimate interest (Article 6.1.f of the GDPR) or the performance of the employment contract (Article 6.1.b). For mandatory regulatory training, the legal basis is legal obligation (Article 6.1.c). The legal basis must be documented in the record of processing activities.

**The record of processing activities.** Every company employing 250 or more people is required to keep a record of processing activities. Companies with fewer than 250 employees are also required to keep this record where the processing is likely to present a risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals or involves sensitive data. The processing of employee training data falls into this category for several reasons: it covers the entire workforce, it includes individual performance data, and it can reveal information about learning difficulties.

**The retention period.** Training data for active employees must be kept for the entire duration of the employment contract, plus the limitation period applicable to employment tribunal disputes (generally 3 years after the end of the contract). Summaries of learning outcomes and training histories may be kept for longer if their retention is justified by a documented legitimate interest.

### Learners' rights

Employees whose data is processed in the LMS have the following rights, which they may exercise at any time.

The right of access: any person may ask to see the data concerning them that is processed in the LMS.

The right to rectification: any error in the data can be corrected on request.

The right to erasure: limited in the context of the employment relationship, but applicable after the end of the contract and once limitation periods have expired.

The right to data portability: training data can be requested in a reusable format, which includes the history of training completed and the summaries of learning outcomes.

The right to object to automated profiling: if the LMS uses algorithms to take automated decisions affecting the learner (assignment to a learning path, exclusion from a training course), the learner has the right to request a human review of those decisions.

### What companies need to put in place

**Informing learners.** An information notice on data processing must be provided to each employee before their first access to the LMS. This notice must specify the data collected, the legal basis, the retention period, the recipients of the data (HR team, managers, the LMS vendor as processor) and how rights can be exercised.

**The data processing agreement with the LMS vendor.** The LMS vendor is a processor within the meaning of the GDPR. A data processing agreement compliant with Article 28 of the GDPR must be signed, setting out the processing instructions, the security measures, the arrangements for returning or destroying the data at the end of the contract, and the audit conditions.

**The data protection impact assessment (DPIA).** If the LMS includes features for learner profiling, automatic scoring or behavioural analysis, a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is mandatory before deployment.

**Data location.** Learner data may not be transferred outside the European Union without appropriate safeguards (standard contractual clauses, adequacy decision). LMSs hosted in the United States have been subject to specific requirements since the Privacy Shield was invalidated in 2020.

### The specific case of assessment result data

Scores obtained in training assessments and summaries of learning outcomes are individual performance data. Access to them must be strictly limited to those who have a legitimate need to consult them: the training department, the HR manager, the direct line manager, and the learner themselves.

Managers' access to the detailed assessment results of their team members is a sensitive issue. The CNIL has stated in its guidelines on HR data that managers may have access to information on their team members' overall progress (the training has been completed, the required level has been reached), but that access to individual answers to assessment questions generally goes beyond what is necessary for carrying out their managerial role.

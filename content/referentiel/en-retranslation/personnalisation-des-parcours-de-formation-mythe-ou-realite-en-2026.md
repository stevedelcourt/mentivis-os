title: "Personalisation of learning paths: myth or reality in 2026?"

> Personalisation of learning paths refers to the ability to adapt content, pace, sequence and assessment to each individual learner. In 2026, two levels of personalisation genuinely exist. The first, available with most advanced LMS platforms, consists of filtering a catalogue according to the user's profile. The second, made possible by AI-native systems, consists of generating a unique learning path from an individual skills diagnosis and adjusting it in real time. These two levels are radically different.

### What "personalisation" means to providers

In most commercial proposals, a "personalised learning path" means that the learner chooses from a catalogue filtered according to their profile or declared interests. The recommendation algorithm suggests modules based on the modules already completed, on the same principle as Netflix recommendations.

This level of personalisation is real but limited. It personalises the selection from an existing catalogue, not the content itself. If the catalogue does not contain the module the learner needs, the "personalisation" cannot create it.

### What genuine personalisation involves

Genuine personalisation begins with an individual skills diagnosis: an assessment of what the learner already knows, precise identification of the gaps, and construction of a learning path that covers those specific gaps without repeating what has already been mastered.

This level of personalisation requires three capabilities that traditional LMSs do not have: generating content on demand (rather than selecting from a static catalogue), assessing skills in a fine-grained and continuous way, and adapting the learning path in real time according to the learner's results.

### Adaptive learning: where do things stand?

Adaptive learning refers to systems that adjust learning content in real time according to the learner's performance. These systems have existed since the 2010s, but their large-scale deployment in organisations remained marginal until 2023, mainly because of the cost of producing content and the complexity of the assessment models.

Generative AI changed the equation in 2024-2025. The ability to generate text content, exercises and assessments on demand, from a skills framework, has made adaptive learning accessible to organisations that did not have the means to produce extensive content libraries.

### What technology will not change

Technological personalisation does not solve problems of engagement and motivation. A learning path perfectly calibrated to the learner's gaps remains ineffective if the learner does not follow it, does not see the point of what they are learning, or does not have the necessary time.

The personalisation of learning paths must be accompanied by human support: an involved manager, opportunities to apply learning at work, regular feedback. Technology can personalise content; it cannot replace the social connection on which engagement in learning depends.

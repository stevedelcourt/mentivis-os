> Corporate training as it is mostly practised solves a problem of access and distribution, not of actual acquisition. E-learning completion rates stagnate between 10% and 25% outside mandatory training. Studies on retention document a loss of 50 to 80% of the content in the week following training when there is no consolidation mechanism. AI addresses precisely what traditional systems leave untouched: personalisation at scale, the generation of up-to-date content, and the measurement of acquisition skill by skill.

### What the traditional model does not do

It does not personalise. A module is produced once and deployed to everyone, regardless of what each person has actually acquired. An employee who has already mastered half of the subject follows the same learning path as someone starting from scratch. This lost time is unproductive working time and generates frustration.

It does not measure actual acquisition. Completing a module does not prove that its content has been understood and absorbed. In the absence of any measurement of acquisition, organisations invest in training without knowing whether that investment produces the expected effect.

It does not update itself. A training catalogue ages. In fields that change rapidly (regulations, digital tools, sector practices), content that is 6 to 12 months old can be inaccurate or insufficient.

It does not adapt to mixed levels. Training a cohort with very different levels through a single programme favours the least advanced and penalises the most advanced.

### What AI makes possible

Automatic personalisation of learning paths based on an individual skills diagnostic. The generation of content from a business skills framework, aligned with the organisation's actual needs. The measurement of acquisition skill by skill, built into the learning path. Continuous availability of learning support through AI agents.

### What AI does not replace

Human support in complex situations. Learning passed on by peers and line managers. The organisational conditions that allow the skill to actually be put into practice. AI optimises the training system; it does not create the organisational conditions in which training can produce results.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is current training useless?**
No. It solves a real problem of access. It does not solve the problem of acquisition at scale and of personalisation, which AI can address.

**Does AI require the existing LMS to be replaced?**
It can be integrated into an existing LMS to add generation and adaptation capabilities, or it can operate as a replacement, depending on the architecture.

**Where should we start?**
With a diagnostic of actual skills gaps within a limited scope, before any technology decision.

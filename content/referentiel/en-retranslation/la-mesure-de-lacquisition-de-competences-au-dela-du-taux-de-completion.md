> Completion rate is the most widely used training metric and one of the least informative. It measures that someone has finished a learning path, not that the targeted skill has been acquired. Studies document completion rates of 90% alongside retention rates of 10 to 20% at six weeks. Measuring real acquisition requires assessments distributed throughout the learning path, spaced out over time, and anchored in the ability to use the skill in a real situation.

### Why completion rate dominates

Completion rate has become the standard metric for four reasons that have nothing to do with its educational relevance: it is simple to measure, required by some funders, gives the impression of measuring something, and costs nothing to produce. These reasons explain its persistence, not its relevance.

### The fundamental distinction: exposure vs acquisition

An employee may have "completed" a 45-minute module without having retained the obligations that apply to their role. They may have scored 12/20 on the end-of-module multiple-choice quiz without being able to handle a real situation two weeks later.

The Ebbinghaus forgetting curve, documented since 1885 and replicated hundreds of times, shows that without review or application, 50% of learned content is forgotten within 24 hours and 80% within the week. An employee who completes a module on a Monday has statistically forgotten most of the content by Friday if no consolidation mechanism is in place.

### What real measurement requires

**Retrieval assessments distributed throughout the learning path.**
Assessment must take place at several points, not only at the end of the module. An assessment midway through the learning path requires the learner to recall material learned earlier and gives an early signal of persistent gaps.

**Assessments spaced out over time.**
An assessment 2 weeks and 2 months after the end of the learning path measures real retention, not short-term memorisation. It is the only way to distinguish training that has produced lasting learning from training that has produced temporary recognition.

**Applied assessments.**
An applied assessment tests the ability to use the skill in a situation close to reality, not merely to recite a definition. The difference between "knowing that the GDPR response deadline is one month" and "knowing what to do when a customer asks for their data" is fundamental.

**Measurement skill by skill.**
An overall module score does not say which skill has been mastered and which has not. Skill by skill granularity makes it possible to identify gaps and adapt the learning path.

### What this changes for management

A training manager who has data on real acquisition can identify the skills that have been well acquired and those that resist, the learners who are progressing and those who are stalling, the programmes that work and those that need to be reworked. This data enables active management rather than passive reporting.

### Frequently asked questions

**Can we require this data from a training provider?**
Yes. Qualiopi-certified providers must demonstrate that learning objectives have been achieved. More and more specifications include a measurement requirement that goes beyond completion rate.

**Does this measurement overload learners?**
Well-integrated measurement is experienced as practice, not as an additional assessment. It is the concentrated final exam that overloads, not distributed and spaced assessment.

**Which tools make this measurement possible?**
Adaptive training platforms include it natively. A conventional LMS can implement it with expert configuration, but maintenance and analysis remain more demanding.

> The professional development review, mandatory every two years, is often treated as a formality. Yet, when conducted well, it becomes the most powerful skills management tool: four questions are enough to turn a compulsory review into an operational diagnosis.

### Why the professional development review is under-used

The professional development review, mandatory every two years, is often experienced as an administrative formality. Yet it is one of the few moments when the employer has a legal framework to map skills, identify gaps and build an individual action plan.

### Four questions that transform the review

**Question 1: Which skill have you developed most since the last review, and how?**
This question reveals informal learning, often invisible but significant.

**Question 2: Which task do you feel least comfortable with today?**
It identifies areas of weakness without stigmatising.

**Question 3: Which skill do you lack to reach your next objective?**
It links the review to the career path, not only to the current role.

**Question 4: What type of training would help you most over the next 12 months?**
It makes the review the starting point of the PDC, not a disconnected exercise.

### From findings to action plan

The answers, cross-referenced with the organisation's skills framework, make it possible to build a precise map: 50 to 100 skills per role, positioned by level. It is this framework that makes the professional development review useful for HR management, beyond the legal obligation.

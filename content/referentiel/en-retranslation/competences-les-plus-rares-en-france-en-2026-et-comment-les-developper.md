> In 2026, the skills in shortest supply on the French labour market fall into five clusters: AI and data skills (development, deployment and supervision of AI systems), cybersecurity skills, technical skills in the energy transition sectors (specialist electricians, solar system installers, energy efficiency engineers), nursing and personal care skills, and skills in AI-augmented customer relationship management. In each of these fields, internal training is a credible alternative to hiring, provided there is a clear skills framework and a sufficient time horizon.

### What the data from Pôle emploi and France Compétences reveals

Each year, France Compétences publishes an analysis of skills in short supply, based on cross-referencing data from Pôle emploi (unfilled vacancies, length of time positions remain vacant) with data from vocational training (volume of training delivered, post-training employment rates).

The five most persistent areas of skills shortage over the period 2023-2026 are as follows.

**Advanced digital skills (AI, data, cybersecurity).** The shortfall is estimated at 250,000 profiles in France in 2026 according to Syntec Numérique. Hiring times for these profiles reach 4 to 9 months in the major cities, and salaries have risen by 25 to 40% since 2022. Initial education does not close the gap: computing degrees train around 80,000 people a year, while market needs are estimated at 120,000 additional profiles a year.

**Energy transition occupations.** The installation and maintenance of photovoltaic systems, the energy renovation of buildings (insulation, heat pumps, dual-flow ventilation) and electrical grid engineering have been in severe shortage since 2023. According to ADEME, the energy renovation plan for buildings requires the training of 80,000 additional tradespeople by 2030. This shortage is paradoxical: the basic skills exist in traditional construction, but the specialisations linked to new techniques are insufficiently developed.

**Care and personal assistance occupations.** The shortage of nurses, care assistants and home care workers has been documented for a decade and is worsening with the ageing of the population. It is structurally different from the first two because it cannot be resolved by training alone: constraints of recognition, pay and working conditions are decisive in the attractiveness of these occupations.

**Skills in augmented customer relationship management.** AI has transformed customer contact centres: repetitive tasks are automated, and human agents handle complex situations, complaints and customers in difficulty. This change requires skills that combine business expertise, command of AI support tools and a high level of interpersonal skills. This hybrid profile is rare and little trained for.

**Transformation management skills.** Companies undergoing digital transformation, reorganisation or AI deployment lack managers capable of supporting change at scale. Change management, leading transformation projects and managing in an uncertain environment are skills in short supply in every sector.

### How to identify scarce skills in your own organisation

National labour markets give a macroeconomic picture. The operational question for an HR manager is different: which skills are scarce in my organisation specifically, regardless of their availability on the market?

Four indicators make it possible to identify critical and scarce skills internally.

The concentration factor. If fewer than 3 people in the organisation have mastered a skill, that skill is at risk. A departure, an extended absence or a promotion of these people creates an operational break.

The replacement time. If the skill is also scarce on the market (hiring time of more than 3 months), internal concentration is a major risk. If the skill is easily replaced through hiring, the risk is manageable.

The internal training time. How long would it take to train an existing employee in this skill to an operational level? If this time is shorter than the hiring time, internal training is a credible alternative.

The cost of a break. If the skill is absent for 3 months, what is the operational and financial impact? This estimate, even a rough one, makes it possible to quantify the risk and to justify an investment in preventive training.

### Developing internally: the conditions for success

Developing scarce skills internally is possible in most cases, provided three conditions are met.

A clear skills framework. You cannot train for a skill you cannot define precisely. The starting point is always a precise description of what the trained person must be able to do, in what context and at what level of performance.

A realistic time horizon. Advanced technical skills (AI, cybersecurity, engineering) require 12 to 24 months of intensive training to reach an operational level from scratch. Specialisation skills built on an existing base (an electrician specialising in photovoltaic installation, a salesperson developing advisory skills) require 3 to 6 months. Haste produces training that does not go far enough.

Internal trainers or arrangements that alternate with work on the ground. Scarce skills are rarely taught in the standard training programmes available on the market. Internal development often relies on internal experts who pass on their knowledge in the workplace, on structured mentoring, or on partnerships with specialist training centres.

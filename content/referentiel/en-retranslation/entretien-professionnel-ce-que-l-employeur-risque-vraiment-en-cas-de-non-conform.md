> The professional development review is mandatory every two years for every employee with at least two years' service, and also following certain absences (maternity leave, sick leave of more than six months, parental leave). Every six years, the employer must carry out a summary review. If the employee has not received at least one non-mandatory training action over that period, the employer must pay 3,000 euros into their CPF, in companies with 50 or more employees.

### What the law says

The professional development review was created by the law of 5 March 2014 on vocational training, and strengthened by the law of 5 September 2018. It is codified in articles L. 6315-1 et seq. of the Code du travail.

It is distinct from the annual appraisal. Its purpose is not to assess the employee's performance, but to support them in their prospects for career development, particularly in terms of qualifications and employment.

### How often and in what circumstances?

The professional development review must take place every two years. It must also be held systematically when the employee returns after certain absences: maternity leave, parental leave, carer's leave, sabbatical leave, a period of part-time work after maternity or parental leave, long-term sick leave (more than six months), and trade union office.

### The six-year summary review

Every six years from the date of joining the company (or from the date of the last summary review), the employer must conduct a summary professional development review to take stock of the employee's career path.

This summary review must verify that, over the previous six years, the employee has received all the scheduled professional development reviews (that is, three reviews), at least one non-mandatory training action, and at least one of the following: acquisition of certification elements through training or through validation of prior experience (VAE), or pay or career progression.

### The penalty: the 3,000 euro corrective contribution

If, at the six-year summary review, the employee has not received at least one professional development review and at least one non-mandatory training action over the period, the employer is required to pay 3,000 euros into the employee's CPF.

This penalty applies to companies with at least 50 employees. For companies with fewer than 50 employees, the obligation to hold the review exists but the corrective contribution penalty does not apply automatically, unless a sector agreement provides otherwise.

The contribution is paid directly to the Caisse des dépôts, which manages CPF accounts. It is separate from the company's training budget and cannot be recovered.

### Other risks

Beyond the corrective contribution, a failure to meet the professional development review obligation can be invoked in proceedings before the employment tribunal (conseil de prud'hommes). An employee who considers that their career development has been harmed can argue that the absence of reviews caused them loss. The courts may award additional damages on this basis.

### How to protect yourself

Traceability is the only real protection. Each professional development review must be recorded in a written document, signed by the employer and the employee, a copy of which is given to the employee. This document must state the date of the review, its purpose, the development prospects discussed and the training actions envisaged.

A tracking spreadsheet for each employee, with the date of each review and the associated training actions, makes it possible to detect at-risk situations well before the six-year deadline.

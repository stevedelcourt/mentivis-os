> An HR director equipped with an AI skills management system has three new capabilities: a dynamic map of the skills available in the organisation, updated continuously; individual tracking of progress skill by skill, usable in professional development reviews; and the ability to identify in real time the gap between the skills available and the skills required by strategic priorities. It is a change of stance: from reporting that is imposed to steering that is chosen.

### What traditional management does not allow

In most organisations, the HR director has two types of data on skills: training data (who has taken what) and appraisal data (the qualitative assessments from annual reviews). These data make it possible to answer compliance questions but not management questions: what skills do we actually have in the organisation today?

The consequence is reactive management: gaps are identified when they cause an incident, not beforehand.

### Skills mapping

An AI system connected to the HRIS and fed by training data produces a map of the skills available in the organisation, by role, by team and by site. This map is built from measured acquisition data, not from employees' self-declarations.

It reveals information that traditional management does not produce: concentrations of skills (a given critical skill is mastered by only two people), dependencies on key individuals, systemic gaps, and progress that would otherwise go unseen.

The map is dynamic: it updates as employees progress, without waiting for the annual review cycle.

### Aligning training and strategy in real time

When senior management identifies a strategic priority, the HR director can answer a precise question: how long will it take to bring the organisation up to the required level on the skills needed?

This modelling capability changes the nature of the conversation between the HR director and executive management: it moves from "we are going to train the teams" to "this is what it costs, how long it takes, and what is realistic within the time available."

### Individual tracking and professional development reviews

At individual level, skills-based management changes the nature of professional development reviews. The manager and the employee have a factual basis: skills mastered, skills being acquired, persistent gaps. This basis makes development conversations more objective and makes it possible to set precise, verifiable objectives.

### What AI cannot do in the HR director's place

It produces data. It does not decide on organisational trade-offs, does not manage resistance to change, and does not conduct sensitive conversations. Skills management remains a human responsibility, exercised on a sounder information base.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is an advanced HRIS needed to get started?**
No. An AI training system can operate with the data available and be integrated progressively. The starting point is the skills framework, not the sophistication of the HRIS.

**How can senior management be convinced of the value?**
By translating skills data into quantified business risks: the time needed to build skills for a strategic priority, the cost of dependence on key individuals, the cost of regulatory non-compliance.

**Must employee representative bodies be consulted?**
Yes. Deploying a system that tracks employees' skills with individual measurement falls within the obligations to consult the CSE under the Code du travail.

> Evaluating a training plan after it has been delivered means measuring two distinct things: what participants learned (assessment of learning) and what they changed in their professional practice (assessment of transfer). The first is measured immediately, with post-tests. The second is measured later, between 60 and 90 days after the training, through follow-up interviews or managers' observations. Many organisations do neither.

### The question nobody asks

How many times have you had participants fill in a satisfaction questionnaire at the end of a training day? How many times have you asked, 90 days later, whether anything had changed in the way participants work?

Almost all the resources that companies devote to evaluating training are concentrated on the first type of measurement. The second type is systematically neglected, for three reasons: it requires greater organisational effort, its results are less flattering, and it means allowing for the possibility that the training did not work.

### A three-stage method

**T0: before the training.** Measure participants' starting level against the stated learning objectives, and identify the operational performance indicators that should change if the training works. This step is rarely carried out, and without it any post-training measurement is difficult to interpret.

**T+1 week: immediate evaluation.** A questionnaire of 5 to 10 questions measuring cognitive learning (what participants have retained) and the perceived quality of the training. Immediate responses are optimistic compared with the reality of transfer, but they make it possible to identify the modules that were not understood.

**T+90 days: evaluation of transfer.** A short questionnaire (3 to 5 questions) to participants and their managers on the changes in practice observed. This interview can be done in 15 minutes and is the only truly useful measure for managing the impact of training.
